1Introduction

What does it mean for a bank to be Shariah compliant?

Shariah compliance is the defining feature that separates Islamic banking from its conventional counterpart. It is not a marketing label — it is a full framework of principles, contracts, and oversight bodies built around what Islam permits (halal) and prohibits (haram) in financial dealings.

يَا مَعْشَرَ التُّجَّارِ: لَا يَبِيعُ فِي سُوقِنَا مَنْ لَا يَفْقَهُ، وَإِلَّا أَكَلَ الرِّبَا رَضِيَ أَمْ أَبَى

"O traders! Do not trade in our market for those who do not possess knowledge (of Islamic commercial law); because he may consume riba willingly or unwillingly (due to lack of knowledge)."

Umar bin Al-Khattab (RA), addressing the traders of Madinah
"Allah has destroyed usury and will give increase for sadaqat." Al-Baqarah: 276

Halal – Haram Foundation

Every Islamic financial product must sit clearly on the halal side of the line — free from riba (usury), gharar (excessive uncertainty), and maysir (gambling). This is the starting filter for any product before it can be offered to customers.

The Barakah Concept

Islamic finance philosophy links growth to blessing: Az-Ziadah (increase) + As-Sa'adah (wellbeing/happiness) = Barakah. Wealth is not just about the numbers growing — it must also bring ethical, spiritual good to society.

2.2 The Shariah Governance Framework

Shariah Advisory Council (SAC)

At the national level, Bank Negara Malaysia's Shariah Advisory Council is the highest authority on Islamic finance matters — its rulings bind all Islamic financial institutions in the country under IFSA 2013.

In-House Shariah Committee

Each bank has its own Shariah Committee that reviews and approves every product, contract, and policy before launch, and conducts ongoing Shariah audits to catch non-compliance early.

•Regulatory Timeline

Evolution of Shariah governance in Malaysia

Malaysia's Shariah compliance framework did not appear overnight — it was built up over four decades of regulation, each layer closing gaps found in the one before it.

1983

Islamic Banking Act (IBA)

Malaysia's first dedicated legislation for Islamic banking, enabling Bank Islam Malaysia Berhad — the country's first full-fledged Islamic bank.

1997

Shariah Advisory Council (SAC) Established

BNM forms the SAC as the highest Shariah authority for Islamic finance, later given binding legal status under the Central Bank of Malaysia Act 2009.

2010

Shariah Governance Framework (SGF)

BNM introduces a formal governance structure requiring every Islamic financial institution to have its own Shariah Committee and internal Shariah review/audit functions.

2013

Islamic Financial Services Act (IFSA)

Consolidates Islamic banking and takaful regulation, giving SAC rulings explicit legal force over Malaysian courts and arbitrators.

2019

Shariah Governance Policy Document (SGPD)

Supersedes the 2010 SGF, strengthening board oversight, Shariah Committee independence, and a bank-wide "Shariah compliance culture" — effective 1 April 2020.

3Critical Analysis

Where Shariah compliance is being tested today

Two pressure points stand out in current literature: the rise of digital finance, and an internal debate about whether the industry is truly Shariah-based, or merely Shariah-compliant on paper.

Digital & Fintech Pressure

Onboarding, e-wallets, robo-advisory, and crowdfunding platforms move faster than traditional Shariah review cycles. Studies flag uneven digital literacy, cybersecurity exposure, and the difficulty of embedding Shariah checks into automated, real-time systems as live challenges for Islamic fintech.

Compliant vs Based

Scholars distinguish "Shariah-compliant" (meets the minimum legal form) from "Shariah-based" (built from Islamic principles from the ground up). Critics argue some products use contract structures like tawarruq mainly to replicate conventional loans in form, raising questions about whether substance matches form.

•Real-Life Application

Shariah-compliant products in the Malaysian market

These are live retail products from a Malaysian Islamic bank, each named after the Shariah contract that makes it halal.

Contract: Mudharabah

Premier Mudharabah Account-i

A current account where profit from the bank's investment of the deposit is shared with the customer under an agreed profit-sharing ratio — instead of paying fixed interest.

Contract: Commodity Murabahah

Islamic Savings Account-i

An online savings account structured on a cost-plus-sale (Murabahah) of a tradable commodity, avoiding riba while still giving the customer a predictable return.

Contract: Tawarruq

Commodity Murabahah Term Financing-i

Business financing built on two genuine sale contracts over a Shariah-compliant commodity — a structure regulated under BNM's own Tawarruq Policy Document.

4Real Case / Current Issue

Shariah non-compliance (SNC) income disclosure in Malaysia

Even well-established Islamic banks occasionally record income from transactions later found non-compliant. What matters, from a Shariah governance view, is how transparently it is disclosed and purified.

RM19.2m
Total Shariah non-compliant income reported by Malaysian Islamic banks, 2015–2020
2016
Year with the highest reported SNC income across the study period

What this shows

Under BNM's Shariah Governance Framework, banks must identify, disclose, and channel any Shariah non-compliant income to charity rather than keep it as profit. Disclosure levels vary between institutions — some banks reported zero SNC events across the period, which researchers note may reflect either stronger controls or less rigorous internal detection. This makes SNC disclosure itself a useful lens for judging how seriously a bank treats Shariah governance, not just whether problems occur.
